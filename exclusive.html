Why I believe the world would be better if sentient life had never existed -- 08/26/2026
For starters, I’ve always been through very unfair situations where I was just too young to understand, and those situations profoundly influenced my way of seeing life. It also gave me a lot of emotional trauma. And, even today I still face the consequences of those traumatic incidents everyday. I’ve been through religious trauma at the age of 11-12, which ruined me and my way of seeing our society and our world as a whole. I won’t talk about religions here specifically, here, I will focus more on my own view on life itself, which may include delicate themes. First of all, I highly agree with the concept of nihilism, meaning I genuinely think there’s no point in living, life doesn't have a purpose or clear meaning. I’ve been thinking a lot about the question “If life has no inherent or objective meaning, can we still create our own meaning or give our lives a purpose?” To which I'd say that yes, we can give our lives a purpose even if life has no inherent meaning, but I don't think it's worth it. Most people won't achieve the goals they dedicate their lives to, especially if those goals involve becoming famous or accomplishing something significant. And even if someone does achieve exactly what they wanted, it ultimately won't matter in the long run. We all die eventually and end up under the ground, so whatever we accomplish will eventually become insignificant. Life is something huge, it's not just another sentient on earth, so for me a life worth living is a life where we would have a purpose that would actually matter. Which is not the case for most people to have realistic goals to achieve, in this economy in general, most people’s dream is to have a great job in life, you’re most likely to not be able to do so. So for me, yes the absence of an objective purpose matters A LOT. During our life, we suffer but also face happiness. Even if happiness were equally frequent or even more frequent than suffering, I don't believe happiness can justify imposing severe suffering on someone who never consented to exist. So no happiness is very far from compensating for suffering. A lot of people would disagree, because my point of view is extremely pessimistic. I don't really care. Antinalism is something I really want to talk about because I have a strong anti-natalist point of view about life. To me, giving life to one another is the most selfish thing one can do, and it having been normalized since forever is disgusting to me. Forcing life into someone who can’t consent is straight up cruel and I see giving life and imposing suffering, trauma, and death on someone who never consented. The implications of imposing an entire lifetime is a concept I'll never be able to understand. When I mean that sentient life shouldn't exist as a whole I mean it, at first I thought only humans should not exist to avoid any hurt on other sentients like animals or insects. But when I thought about it on a deeper level, I realized that even without humans, the same reproduction phenomenon does happen to give birth to non-consenting babies and also do impose suffering on them. So even without humans, animals would still suffer from forced birth, they would then suffer from the food chain, climate etc; they just wouldn't suffer from abuse or laboratory captivity. Again, for me, giving birth is the most selfish thing one can do, and calling someone selfish for committing suicide is actually one of the biggest bullshit someone can say. Someone ending their life that they DID NOT choose to live is actually the best thing they can do for themselves and the best decisions someone can make I’ve always hated society and humans and general, but if there’s something I hate more than humanity, it's norms. Unspoken expectations are everything, in social life, in relationships, friendships, and it's just too much. Society values work, education, productivity and conformity more than they value mental health, but they won’t understand that if someone has poor mental health, they won’t be as productive. For a system to genuinely be a successful system, it should consider both economic stability and human well-being. I wouldn't call myself an anarchist, but I still share a lot of points of view with its concept. Ever since I was a kid, I just couldn't understand why some people get authority over others, I just couldn't understand hierarchy and inequality. To this day I still struggle with understanding it and why everyone seems to act like it's normal. But as I’ve grown up, I’ve got used to the fact that everything in life is unfair and that we basically can't do anything about it. I genuinely wish I was more optimistic about seeing a real change in our system, but I’ve always been told “life is unfair, deal with it” so it just grew on me. I’ve been thinking about this sentence “but I'm happy, so life is good” for weeks, and if I'm giving my blunt opinion about it, it's pure bullshit. You being happy doesn't mean our system is at least the bare minimum, or that people around you aren’t dying because our president refuses to go to war by themselves, or that people around you aren't struggling with their trauma everyday. I’ve always had a very black or white way to see life. So no, for me life isn't great with some bad sides, the bad sides clearly counterforce the great ones. My relationships with people in general have always been complicated, as long as I can remember, I've always felt like others were strange or difficult to understand, and that no matter how hard I try I can’t seem to fit in. I’ve invariably struggled with reciprocating affection as a whole and I constantly feel a deep sense of separation from the people around me. Ultimately, this persistent disconnect highlights a profound sense of alienation. After my religious trauma, my sense of life as a whole changed drastically, meaning I couldn't tell what was real and what wasn't, couldn't tell good from bad and had a huge dehumanization episode. But having gone through that at such a young age, as mentioned earlier, still affects me, and I still can’t feel like anything really matters or is really real. I still can’t tell if the people around me are conscious. Sometimes, even though I feel like I don't have consciousness, I’ve been feeling like I'm observing people’s life instead of participating. To simply explain what I talked about at the start, I wish any sentient mind wouldn't exist, but as we “can’t” do anything about it, I just wished our system would at least work differently.

The song name, “Take the form” isn't literal but a metaphor, to me it would mean something close to “become something or someone else that I want you to be” (due to the fact that it's someone “telling” the other to take the form.), “The form” being something or someone the person maybe doesn’t want to be. We can conclude that this song will deal with themes like forcing someone and manipulation. The first line is very straight forward “I look at you with the irises of lust, I'd love to spend the night but there's no trust“ I interpret this line as the narrator saying that they want to get sexual with someone who doesn't trust them, which is explicitly said, but I want to focus more on the fact that it’s the very first line. This could mean the person saying that would focus more on the sexual side of love rather the affectionate side. “So you turn me into a marble bust, And smash me into a sea of dust” Could mean that the narrator got his sexual needs denied by the girl. Focusing more on “smash me into a sea of dust” is a very brutal way to explain his feelings, meaning it wasn't just a simple rejection but something deeper to him. Then we got the chorus “If I can do no harm, then I can do no good”, this has to be my favorite line in this whole song, it means so much in so little words. The word anyways here means that they don't think much about what they just did and are just brushing it off before talking about something else. They clearly say that they don't fear hoping too much or lying, which makes us see the narrator as a person who’s more mean than kind, this could go with the chorus when I mentioned that they feel hopeless because they can't be good or bad. The next lyrics are much more sexual “We are in tandem we are in sync like, When you were in the airplane being poetic” imply that the narrator and the person got sexual in the end, “we are in sync”, but I want to focus more on “When you were in the airplane being poetic” because this is the most romantic line the narrator said so far, and I'm noticing that he is saying it NOW that they are getting sexual, this could imply that the narrator is unable to feel romantic emotions and is only searching for sex, it mean that he can feel those feelings but only when he’s getting what he wants (sex). “Up there in the pink, You just need to live up there in your mind” this is very vague but I would interpret “up there” as the woman being on top during sex but also thinking about romantic things, the line “You just need to live up there in your mind, that’s what I think” would mean the man knows that if the girl is thinking about all sort of romantic stuff with him she’ll let him have sex with her, I interpret the narrator as a manipulator toward her. “So bring our glasses together, clink” This would mean that the man is proud of his manipulation technique and is celebrating it. “Do it for me and keep us warm, Become real, take the form, Do it for me, babe, take the form“ those lines are very deep and well written, especially because they are the very last lyrics.
